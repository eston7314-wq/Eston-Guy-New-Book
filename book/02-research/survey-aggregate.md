# Survey aggregate: transcription of the thesis charts

Prepared by book-strategist, 2026-11-10, from `book/source/images/image2.png` to `image27.png`
(the charts in thesis chapter 6; `image1.jpeg` is the university logo). Figure numbers and
captions are from `book/source/thesis-2025.md` (lines given). Question wording and scale anchors
are from the survey instrument in thesis chapter 5 (lines given).

**This file is the only source the book may use for survey numbers** (config hard rule 10). Use
overall counts and proportions only: the whole group of 50, or by country or sex at most. Never
an individual's answer, quotation, story or identifying detail. Always give the sample size (50).
Never claim the findings represent all people of African heritage. The participants are real
(author, 2026-11-10: "We got 50 replies").

## How the values were read

- The charts are bar charts without printed values. Every count below was **read from the
  chart** against its gridlines. Each bar sits on a whole number, so every value could be read
  to the nearest participant, except where a cell is marked `[UNCLEAR: ...]`.
- Where a chart shows no bar for a response, the count is recorded as 0. In every such case
  the other bars already total 50.
- **Check against Fig.27.** Fig.27 (image27) is a table with one row per participant. I tallied
  eight of its columns and compared them with the charts: five demographic columns (country,
  sex, employment, education, interviewed) and three item columns (Faith Importance, Church
  Mental Health Supportiveness, Community Openness). All eight tallies reproduce the charts
  exactly. I did not re-tally the other 18 item
  columns.
- **Fig.27 is not transcribed here, and must never be reproduced in the book.** It holds
  individual answers next to country, sex, employment and education, which together can
  identify someone (for example, one Canadian participant is the only person in the "College"
  group for that country). Rule 10 forbids its use beyond the aggregate check above.
- **Mapping figures to questions** is my inference from the order of the charts and their
  titles, matched against the order of the questions in thesis 4180-4407. The thesis does not
  state the mapping.
- "Top two" means responses 4 and 5 together; "bottom two" means 1 and 2.

## Inconsistencies and cautions

1. **Church services against community worship (Fig.5 against Fig.8).** Community worship explicitly includes church services (4251-4252). Yet 10 participants chose "daily" for church services and only 7 chose "daily" for community worship. Read literally, the two charts cannot both be right. The likeliest explanation is the out-of-order scale (3 = Daily, 4 = Weekly, 5 = More than once a week; 4203, 4248, 4254): many participants probably read it as a plain low-to-high scale. **Author to check:** were these the anchors on the live Google Form? Until then, the book uses only "never" and "occasionally" from Fig.5, Fig.7 and Fig.8.
2. **Good mental health and frequent stress at once (Fig.10 against Fig.11 and Fig.12).** 38 of 50 rate their mental health over the past month as good or excellent. Yet 39 of 50 say they feel anxiety or stress frequently or almost always, and 37 of 50 felt stress, anxiety or depression often or always in the past year. This is not impossible: the time frames differ, and people can cope well under strain. The book must report both and not pick one. It can also say honestly that this is a pattern worth asking about.
3. **The thesis's key finding against the charts (4658-4663 against Fig.11 and Fig.12).** The thesis reports that people who practised their faith more often reported *lower* anxiety, stress and depression, and that this was statistically significant. The charts show high stress overall and cannot show an association. The SPSS chi-square and regression results (4634-4654) are not reported anywhere in the thesis. **The book may not state the association as a finding** unless the author supplies the output. Even then, with 50 people, it should be described in words, as the thesis reports it, with the sample size.
4. **Openness against stigma (Fig.20 against 4670-4673 and 4716-4721).** 39 of 50 say their Christian community discusses mental health frequently or is very open to it. Yet the thesis reports that "a substantial number" were reluctant to seek help because of stigma (4670-4673). The interviews called for "more open discussions" (4718-4719). And only 23 of 50 call their church supportive about mental health (Fig.19). Being willing to talk and being supportive are different things, so this is a real tension, not an error. The book should name it and not smooth it over.
5. **The stigma and help-seeking finding has no chart.** The yes/no questions that would show it were asked but are not charted: professional mental-health support (4306-4311), support from a faith leader (4313-4314), religious or spiritual counselling (4264-4266), and faith used for emotional support (4339-4342). Age group (4411-4413) and denomination (4214-4220) were asked but not charted either. **Author to check:** do the Google Form results hold these counts? The counts for professional help and faith-leader help would be the most useful numbers in the book.
6. **Two near-identical distributions.** Fig.1, Faith Importance (3/8/6/14/19), and Fig.15, Faith Positive Impact (3/8/6/15/18), differ by one participant in one cell. The questions are close in meaning, so this is plausible. I record it only so that no one later mistakes one chart for the other. The Fig.27 tally confirms Fig.1; I did not tally Fig.15.
7. **Education categories do not match the questionnaire.** The questionnaire offered Secondary School, GCSE, Apprenticeship, A-Level, Graduate/Degree and Other (4428-4431). The chart adds College, Diploma, High School and Technical Certificate, presumably entered under "Other" or adapted for North America. GCSE and Secondary School overlap. The book should not report education levels beyond saying the group was mixed.
8. **Who is missing.** No participant was unemployed, self-employed or retired (Fig.24), and there are no age data. The book must not describe the group as "balanced" or "representative". The thesis uses both words (4625-4626, 4101-4103), and the second conflicts with rule 10 and with the thesis's own limitation (4907-4910).
9. **Faith important to well-being against faith helps.** 11 of 50 disagree that faith is important to their overall well-being (Fig.1). Yet only 3 of 50 put faith's help with mental-health challenges below "possibly" (Fig.13). The wording differs, so this is a caution, not an error. Do not cite Fig.1 and Fig.13 together as if they measured the same thing.
10. **The charts label responses "1-5" only.** Any chart redrawn for the book must print the anchor words from the questionnaire, not the numbers.
