## Change log (line, original / revision / why)

### 1. Line 3, opening: two sentences joined

- **Original:** "Seventy-two people died. Most of them were from minority ethnic groups, among them people of African heritage. A church leader later reported ..."
- **Revision:** "Seventy-two people died, most of them from minority ethnic groups, among them people of African heritage. A church leader later reported ..."
- **Why:** the opening already hooks in its first sentence (the date, the fire, the tower). The role asks for the chapter's question by about sentence four. It came at sentence seven, after six sentences of scene. Joining the two shortest sentences in the pack's own recommended wording (pack 1.3, "Recommended wording frame") brings the church into sentence 3 and the question ("what it takes for a church to be a family that carries people when they cannot carry themselves") one sentence sooner. Nothing else could move without adding to the Grenfell account or cutting it, and pack 1.3 forbids the first and config rule 8 the second, so the question now comes at sentence six. No fact added or dropped.

### 2. New paragraph after line 29 (section 2): the paralysed man carried by four, Mark 2:1-5

- **Original:** the Galatians paragraph ("As I read it, a burden shared among several people is still a burden, but each of them carries less of its weight ... amid the pressures of the wider society.") was followed directly by the heading "Many Black churches have become a family house ...".
- **Revision:** a new paragraph between them (126 words):
  "Mark tells of four people who did that carrying with their own hands. Jesus was teaching in a house in Capernaum so crowded that there was no room even at the door. A paralysed man was brought to him, carried by four. They could not get near for the crowd, so they opened up the roof above the place where Jesus was and let the man down on his bed (Mark 2:1-4). Mark says that Jesus saw the faith of these people before he said a word to the man (Mark 2:5). As I read it, that faith belonged to the ones who did the carrying. The man could not have reached Jesus by himself, and none of the four could have carried him there alone."
- **Why:** this is the chapter's most abstract stretch on its own central idea. The section states the charter (Acts 2, 1 Corinthians 1:12, Galatians 6:2) and the writer's gloss ("each of them carries less of its weight"), but it has no person carrying anyone. Ch05 ended on "a family that carries people when they cannot carry themselves", and Mark 2 is that sentence acted out: a man who cannot come, four who carry him, a crowd in the way, a roof opened. It has a person, a moment and a stake, and it is New Testament (the book's subject), not a composite. The scene stops at the carrying. **Deliberately left out:** the words Jesus then spoke (2:5, "thy sins be forgiven thee"), because linking an illness with sin is ch07's subject and is not this scene's point; and the healing (2:11-12), so that the scene does not promise that a community's faith brings a cure (ch04's guard, "never tell someone who is still ill that they lacked faith"; pack 8.5). **Wording:** the KJV says "borne of four", not "four men" or "friends", so neither is written. "Paralysed" is the modern sense of "sick of the palsy". Paraphrased throughout without quotation marks: the KJV wording is not in the thesis or any recorded reading. **Writer's sentences, for Eston's ear:** the lead-in, "As I read it, that faith belonged to the ones who did the carrying" (a reading of "their faith", which could include the man) and the last sentence.

### 4. New paragraph after line 49 (section 4, the first door): Eli and Hannah, 1 Samuel 1:9-18

- **Original:** the paragraph on what clergy preach and the belonging churches give ("... strengthen the life of these communities and, in my view, promote collective healing.") was followed directly by "A first door is only as good as where it leads."
- **Revision:** a new paragraph between them (136 words):
  "Chapter 5 told how Hannah prayed at Shiloh. The priest there, Eli, was sitting by a post of the temple, and when he saw her lips moving without a sound he took her for drunk. "How long wilt thou be drunken?" he asked her (1 Samuel 1:14). She answered that she had drunk nothing and was praying out of great sorrow (1 Samuel 1:15-16). Eli sent her away in peace with a blessing, "the God of Israel grant thee thy petition" (1 Samuel 1:17), and she went her way and ate. The same priest first misjudged her and then blessed her, and what changed in between was that he heard her out. If you have ever taken something heavy to a pastor, you will know how much depends on which of those two answers you meet."
- **Why:** the first-door section is the chapter's second abstract stretch. It says that clergy are trusted and that people go to them first (Taylor 2000; flags 85 and 80), and it warns that the door must lead somewhere, but it shows no one at the door. Eli is the Bible's plainest picture of a troubled believer meeting a religious leader: he first misreads her distress, then hears her and sends her away in peace. The scene proves the section's point, that what a person meets at that first door matters, without saying anything about any real pastor. It also prepares the bridge ("the church that opens at 3 a.m. can also close its ears") without spending ch07's material. The ch05 story pass left Eli for ch06 or ch07. Hannah is ch05's, so she is called back by chapter and her ch05 quotations are not repeated ("only her lips moved" becomes "her lips moving without a sound"; "poured out my soul" is not used). **Not used:** 1:27 (her petition granted), so the scene promises no answer to prayer. **Writer's sentences, for Eston's ear:** "The same priest first misjudged her and then blessed her, and what changed in between was that he heard her out" and the direct-address close.
