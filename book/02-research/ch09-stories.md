## Change log (line, original / revision / why)

### 1. Line 47, section 2 ("Young Black men are most at risk, and a church can be a bridge to help"): one sentence added before the signs box

- **Original:** "The church, I wrote, has a unique role in suicide prevention, because its influence can reach people at risk before a crisis develops. Believers can learn to recognise signs of distress in themselves and in others, although that will require a change in attitudes to the stigma around mental health."
- **Added at the end:** "You may already have seen such signs in someone near you without knowing what they meant, or what to say: a friend who quietly stopped coming to Bible study, or a relative whose mood changed suddenly and who began to speak about death."
- **Why:** section 2 is the chapter's most lecture-dense stretch (agencies, studies, initiatives, programmes), and no one is in it. The paragraph says believers can recognise distress in others; the sentence lets the reader see it in a scene they may know. **Guards:** the changes it shows are three of the signs the thesis lists (3277-3280), so it adds no clinical content; the box with its help pointer follows at once (safe messaging); it is written so that no bereaved reader is told they should have acted. "Bible study" because the opening and section 4 already use the prayer meeting. **Writer's sentence, for Eston's ear.**

### 2. Line 67, section 3 ("Verses about marriage and forgiveness have been used to keep people in danger"): one sentence added in the forgiveness paragraph

- **Original:** "... and that this has led to a high degree of victim-blaming in some communities. Church leaders may inadvertently perpetuate abuse ..."
- **Revision:** "... in some communities. Perhaps you have heard it said, from a pulpit or in a private word after a service, that a good Christian wife forgives and endures. Church leaders may inadvertently perpetuate abuse ..."
- **Why:** the paragraph asserts that forgiveness has been taught so that victims endure abuse, then reads Matthew 5:38-39. The sentence puts the teaching where a reader would actually hear it, so the KJV reading that follows answers something concrete. **Guards:** its words rest on the thesis ("endure abuse as part of their Christian duty or expectation", 3331-3334) and the blueprint's shift line ("A good Christian wife endures"); nothing is in quotation marks and no one is quoted; the reader is addressed as someone who heard the teaching, not as a victim (no invitation to disclose). The thesis's point that men are abused too (section 4) is untouched. **Writer's sentence, for Eston's ear.**
