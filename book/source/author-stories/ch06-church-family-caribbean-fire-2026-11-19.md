My church story:

A few years ago, the family of a close family friend suffered a catastrophe in a small island in the Caribbean. Our friend's sister, her husband and three children lost their home in a fire that was caused by faulty electrical wiring.

The house was insured, but  emergency relief would have been minimal, so their local church stepped in to help. Friends and close relatives accommodated the family in two separate dwellings. At the same time, our friend discussed the problem with her local Pentecostal Pastor, in London.

He appealed to his congregation for financial support. A total of five thousand and three hundred  pounds were raised over two weeks, and that was sent to the local church in the small Caribbean village. The currency exchange rate brought that total to forty-five  thousand four hundred and eighty dollars in the local currency. That was added to funds raised by relatives, friends and neighbours.

A temporary three bedroom wooden house,,  equipped with basic amenities was constructed at one corner of the family's land within three weeks. Local businesses  had contributed some construction materials, and some of the funds raised was used to finance the building of the temporary structure. Church members provided free labour, over the three weeks. Certain specialist charged only for materials, and their work was done at reduced costs.

The community spirit that brought forth such level of support for the distressed family, was due to an annual harvest festival that the local Pentecostal church promoted and managed. Families, friends and visitors socialised, and raise funds for small community projects and for the church, at the festival.

The two churches liaised, and helped the family through the process of making their insurance claim up to the  rebuilding of their home. Every Sunday, congregants at both churches read passages of scripture, and prayed for the families, for the welfare of all who had helped,  particularly  the church leaders  and personnel who dealt with the administrative issues.

I believe that  the distressed family's faith in God, and the communities readiness to give in Jesus's name, helped them to occupy their new permanent home within eleven months after losing their former home.
