# Style sheet

The house style for the whole manuscript, kept for the build and for a human proofreader.
The copy editor adds to it at each chapter. The same decisions are recorded in `book/book-bible.md`
under "Consistency decisions". If the two ever disagree, the Book Bible wins, and this sheet must be corrected.
Started 2026-11-11 by copy-editor at ch00. Each line gives its source.

## Spelling

- British, -ise (config rule 4): realise, recognise, organise, marginalised, programme, judgement, towards, learned (not learnt), cooperation (no hyphen), worshipping.
- practise (verb), practice (noun). licence (noun), license (verb).
- Proper names keep their own spelling: World Health Organization; US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. British spelling applies to our paraphrase of them.
- Quotations keep their own spelling, capitalisation and hyphenation (Douglass's "recognize" and "class-leaders"; KJV "baptizing"; Prince's "the Moravian church"). House style applies only to our narration and paraphrase ("class leaders") (copy-editor 2026-11-11, ch01; Hart's Rules).
- Possessive of Jesus: "Jesus's" (Hart's Rules, as for any name ending in s); "Christ's". The thesis writes "Jesus'"; quotations keep their own form (ch03, 2026-11-15).

## Punctuation

- No serial (Oxford) comma (config rule 4). If a list becomes ambiguous without it, recast the sentence. A comma before "and" joining two clauses, or before the second of two items, is allowed.
- Quotations keep their own serial commas ("the pure, peaceable, and impartial Christianity of Christ", Douglass). Config rule 4 governs our prose only (ch01).
- Quotation marks: double for quotations and scare quotes, single inside them. The files use straight marks, which are curled at typesetting (the build does not do this yet). Punctuation goes inside the closing mark only if it belongs to the quotation. The citation follows the closing mark. If the source sentence goes on after the quoted words, the full stop goes outside: "...pulled it down twice". (ch01 fact check row 65; source text checked).
- Reported speech and quoted first person: where a quotation is reported in the third person ("she said that she ..."), the quoted span may not switch to the first person ("my soul"). Paraphrase that part outside the marks and keep the KJV words that fit: "she said that she was "a woman of a sorrowful spirit" and had poured out her soul "before the LORD"" (1 Samuel 1:15); or quote the speech directly with its own lead-in (Hart's Rules; copy-editor 2026-11-17, ch05).
- A comma introduces a quotation after a speaking or praying verb: "Jesus prayed, "Father ..."", "Jesus cried, "My God ..."". No comma where the quotation runs on as the object of the sentence ("the verse asks for "prayer and supplication with thanksgiving"") (ch04 and ch05 finals; copy-editor 2026-11-17).
- No em or en dashes in prose (humanizer §8). Number ranges take a hyphen (Luke 8:43-48; 3,500-4,600).
- Abbreviations have no full stops: UK, USA, US, CDC, WHO, PhD, GCSE, KJV, ROI.
- "the USA" and "the UK" take the article as nouns in narration, also inside a list: "in Britain, the USA and Canada". Bare forms only in labels and data shorthand ("UK 20, USA 16") (ch01, 2026-11-13). In a country list that also names Canada, write "the USA", not "America" (which can read as the continent); "America" stays where it stands alone (ch02).
- Titles and contractions have no full stops in narration: Dr, Jr, St, Mr, Mrs (the Reverend Dr Martin Luther King Jr; St Thomas; St George's Methodist Church). Quotations keep theirs (ch01). St has no full stop in place and institution names either: St Augustine Campus, St Mary's College (ch04 story, 2026-11-17).

## Capitalisation

- Headings: H1 chapter titles in title case, preceded by "Chapter N: " with the number in figures, e.g. "Chapter 1: A Faith Handed Down in Chains" (author decision 2026-11-16; applies to every numbered chapter, 1-11; the Preface and the Afterword keep their own labels). H2 and H3 in sentence case. Closer labels are fixed: Reflection and practice; For church leaders; For families; For clinicians; Questions for small groups.
- Specific posts are capitalised (Head of Religious Studies, Head of Year 11, Assistant Headteacher, Heads of Year). The generic role is lowercase (the headteacher, a leader at her church, pastor).
- Named courses and programmes are capitalised: Contemporary Religions (plural; the same subject at university and at school, author 2026-11-10), Religious Studies, the Gifted and Talented programme.
- Institutions are written in full and capitalised: the University of London; Selinus University of Science and Literature. Generic "university" and "school" are lowercase (copy-editor 2026-11-11, ch00).
- Faith terms: the Bible, Bible study, the New Testament, the Eucharist, Psalms (the book), the Gospels (the books), God, Christ. Lowercase: scripture, the psalms (individual psalms), biblical. "Black Church" is used only for the institution (Book Bible 2026-11-07).
- Church: capital C for a denomination or body, or for the institution as a whole (the Church of England, the Moravian Church, the English Church, the African Methodist Episcopal Church, the colonial Church). Lowercase for congregations, buildings and generic use (our churches, Black churches, a small Protestant church, that church, the Moravian chapel) (ch01).
- Black is capitalised for people and their institutions (Black Christians, Black churches, Black theology). white is lowercase in narration (a young white man). It is capitalised only as a study's or census category (White Americans, White British). Quotations keep their own form (ch01).
- indigenous is lowercase as an adjective (Hart's Rules), **pending the author** (Canadian and many US publishers capitalise Indigenous; it is one global switch). Named peoples are capitalised: Arawaks, Caribs, Cree, Iroquois, Ojibwa, Native American (ch01).
- Sabbath school, Sunday school: capitalise the day and lowercase "school". Likewise camp meeting, prayer meeting (ch01; Hart's Rules).
- the civil rights movement is lowercase. Named Acts are capitalised: the Civil Rights Act, the Voting Rights Act, the Slavery Abolition Act (ch01).
- Titles: books and their short forms in italics (*Narrative*, *History*, *The Wretched of the Earth*). Speeches, letters and articles in double quotation marks with title-case capitals ("I Have a Dream", "Letter from Birmingham Jail") (ch01).
- Cross-references: chapter 4 (lowercase mid-sentence, no leading zero; never "chapter 04"); Part One to Part Four; Appendix A, Appendix B; the preface, the afterword.
- Named periods and events are capitalised: the Great Migration, gospel's Golden Age, the Second World War (not World War II), the Windrush generation, the Covid-19 pandemic (Hart's form, not COVID-19) (ch02).
- Song titles in double quotation marks with their own capitals ("Go Down, Moses", "Redemption Song", "Oh Happy Day"); a television or radio series in italics (*My Life, My Religion*). In-copyright lyrics are paraphrased outside quotation marks with the song named (author 2026-11-13) (ch02).
- Named festivals and events take capitals on every word of the name: the Liverpool Music Festival (author 2026-11-14, ch02).
- Seasons are lower case: summer, the summer holidays (Hart's Rules). A season word in a proper name keeps its capital: Spring Gardens (ch01) (ch03, 2026-11-15, the author's lecturer story).
- Named degrees and qualifications: the degree's own name is capitalised, the words "degree" and "honours" are lower case: a Bachelor of Education degree with honours; Certificates in Education (author 2026-11-15, ch03 lecturer story; was "Bachelor in Education Degree with Honours").
- Degrees and posts in the author's stories (copy-editor 2026-11-17, ch04 story; his one-time tidy permission of 2026-11-17): a generic degree is lower case with the apostrophe, "a master's degree" (was "Masters Degree"; as the named-degrees rule above). A specific post named by its title is capitalised: Head of Religious Studies, Lecturer in Education, Training Officer 11 (author 2026-11-17: his form) (Senior Training Officer). A grade or role after "a" or "one of our" is lower case: a staff nurse, one of our consultants. Grade numerals are Roman (Training Officer 11 (author 2026-11-17)).
- "the Ministry" is capitalised as the short form of a named body (the Ministry of Health), as "the Kingdom". Names of schools and colleges capitalise every word, "School" included: Alexandra Park School, Rosary Boys Primary School, Tranquility Boys Secondary School, St Mary's College; "primary education" is lower case (ch04 story, 2026-11-17).
- Music Director (a specific post) is capitalised. "the Headteacher" is capitalised where it names one person in the author's own story and the sentence joined to it; generic "headteacher" stays lowercase (ch02).

## Numbers and dates

- Words for one to ten in ordinary prose. Figures for 11 and above.
- Figures for every count, statistic or survey figure in a data sentence, even when the number is below 11 ("20 in the UK ... 30 women and 20 men"; "215 students").
- Survey proportions: "N of the 50" (Book Bible v2). Never "representative" or "balanced".
- Spell out a number that begins a sentence ("Fifty people...", "Twenty of them...").
- "fifty" in words when it names the group in narrative with no data in the sentence ("what fifty people told me", "the fifty"), to match the ch10 title.
- School years: Year 11. Dates: 21 April 2025 (day month year, no comma, no ordinal).
- Centuries in words, lowercase: the sixteenth century, early in the seventeenth century. Hyphenate before a noun: eighteenth-century missions. Decades in figures with no apostrophe: the 1830s. Not "the 1700s" for a century (ch01).
- Phone numbers are written as the service writes them: 116 123; 988; 9-8-8.
- "per cent" in running text, "%" only in tables and figures: "around 60 per cent worse on average" (first used ch05, the thesis's Larcom figure; Hart's Rules). Was "proposed, not yet used" (copy-editor 2026-11-17, ch05). **Note 2026-11-17:** the 60 per cent example was cut by the author (open-claims 71, ch05 cut-list row 12); the rule stands, and the book has no "per cent" in running text yet.
- Round numbers in narrative follow the same rule: "20 African men", "more than 400 years", "more than 20 years" (ch02, 2026-11-14).
- **Author exception (author 2026-11-15, ch03):** in the author's own story the numbers stay as he chooses, even where the rule above would give figures: "a group of seventeen", "the group of twenty-five" (ch03 lecturer story; "the top five" follows the rule anyway). The exception covers his story text only; the rule stands for all other prose, including the writer's joining sentences. Ask him before applying the figures rule to another of his stories.
- Numbers from a Bible narrative stay in words, as the KJV has them, where the narration retells the passage: "for twelve years", "a girl of twelve", "the ten lepers", "the nine", "the feeding of the five thousand", "five loaves and two fishes", "prayed three times". Source: the KJV verses quoted beside them, and ch03's "the sixth hour" (John 4:6). The figures rule stands for all other prose and for every count or survey figure (copy-editor 2026-11-16, ch04).
- Era dates: "AD 64", "AD 70" (AD before the figure, no full stops; Hart's Rules); BC after the figure if ever needed ("64 BC"). Never "B.C.E." (ch04).
- "the twelve" (the disciples Jesus chose, Luke 6:13) is lower case and in words in narration, as the KJV has it ("of them he chose twelve"); never "the Twelve". Applies the Bible-narrative numbers rule (copy-editor 2026-11-17, ch05).

## Scripture

- Translation: the King James Version (KJV) throughout (author decision 2026-11-13).
- Reference form: book name in full, chapter:verse, translation after a comma, all in one pair of parentheses after the quotation: "thy faith hath made thee whole" (Luke 8:48). No abbreviated book names (Hebrews, not Heb.; 1 Corinthians, not 1 Cor.).
- One translation is now chosen (KJV, 2026-11-13): the copyright page names it, KJV quotations carry no translation tag ("thy faith hath made thee whole" (Luke 8:48)), and only a quotation from another version is marked.
- "The writer to the Hebrews", not "Paul", for Hebrews (Book Bible 2026-11-07).
- Every quotation in the body has a reference, including a verse whose book the prose names ("(Leviticus 19:18)") and a verse that follows one just cited ("(Luke 15:18)" after 15:17). A run of quotations from one passage under a named Gospel may share one reference after its last quotation ("(Mark 12:31)", "(Luke 23:33-34)"). Quotations repeated in the closer from the body need none. If the verse goes on past the quoted words, the full stop follows the reference: `thy people" (Leviticus 19:18).` (ch03, 2026-11-15; fact check N2).
- Parables are named with capitals and "parable" is lower case: the Good Samaritan, the Sower, the Prodigal Son, the Lost Son; "the parable of the Sower". People in a parable are lower case (the father, the elder brother, a priest, a Levite, the lawyer); "the Samaritan" is capitalised as the name of a people (ch03).
- Named teachings and texts are capitalised: the Sermon on the Mount, the Lord's Prayer, the Law of Moses, the Gospels, Mark's Gospel (ch01 "Matthew's Gospel"). gospel music and gospel hymns stay lower case (ch02). A letter is described in lower case in narration ("the first letter to the Corinthians") and given in full in references (1 Corinthians 15:55, 57) (ch03).
- Kingdom of God: capitalised in narration, and its short form too ("the message of the Kingdom"); the KJV's lower case inside quotations ("the kingdom of God", "the kingdom of heaven") (thesis 1738-1740; author 2026-11-13; ch03).
- Several references in one parenthesis are separated by semicolons, and a book is named once for the references from it that follow: "(Matthew 9:22; Mark 5:34; 10:52; Luke 8:48; 17:19)", "(Romans 1:4; 4:25; 1 Peter 1:21)", "(Acts 2:24; 3:15; 10:40; Ephesians 1:20)". Verses in one chapter keep the comma form "(Luke 15:24, 32)". Book names are never abbreviated (copy-editor 2026-11-16, ch04).
- apostle: lower case as a common noun ("the letters of the apostles"); capitalised as a title before a name or standing for one ("the Apostles Paul, Peter and John"; "this John was the Apostle"). "the Apostles' Creed" is a proper name. "the early Church" takes the capital (the institution as a whole, ch01 Church rule) (ch04).

## Names

- Initials are spaced, with full stops: Eston T. B. Guy; Father F. W. G. Perkins; H. Gould; T. Hartney. Professor Salvatore Fava; Pope Francis.
- Accents and other diacritics in names are kept, in prose and in notes: Junípero Serra, Medellín, Gustavo Gutiérrez, Bécares, González (Hart's Rules). Names that have a common English form stay without the accent (Quebec) (ch01).
- Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953; 2009 ed.), never "(1889)" (Book Bible v2).
- Studies and books named in running text (copy-editor 2026-11-17, ch05; author's answer on open-claims 70, "Smith, McCullough and Poll"): a study is "A study by" plus every author's surname, up to four, joined with commas and "and", then the year in parentheses: "Smith, McCullough and Poll (2003)", "Iyer, Larcom, Shah and She (2025)". No "et al", "and colleagues" or "&" in prose (no serial comma). A book: short title in italics, year in parentheses, author by the name the thesis uses: "In *Faith and Mental Health* (2009), as I understand it, Harold G. Koenig ...", as "*Personal Knowledge* (1958)" (ch05 line 51). Journal, volume, publisher and DOI go in the notes and `book/02-research/sources.md`, not the prose.
- Samaritans (no "the" before it in the help-line note); 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline.

## Terms and hyphenation

- African heritage (umbrella term). Before a noun it is open, never hyphenated: "African heritage Christians" (like African American; thesis 3983, 4063). Prefer "of African heritage" after the noun where it reads naturally, but do not rewrite the attributive form (copy-editor 2026-11-11, ch01). African American, African British, African Canadian: no hyphen, as noun or adjective. Afro-Caribbean and mixed-race: hyphenated.
- mental health (noun); mental-health (before a noun: mental-health services). Likewise public-health bodies, long-term physical illness.
- postgraduate (closed); post-registration (hyphenated) (ch04 story, 2026-11-17).
- well-being (always hyphenated). daughter-in-law. headteacher (one word).
- health care (noun), health-care (before a noun). class leader (open, in narration). large-scale, all-powerful, non-violent, African-initiated before a noun (ch01).
- Survey wording: "50 participants", "overall numbers" (config rule 10).
- diaspora: lower case in narration ("the African diaspora", "the diaspora", "Christians in the African diaspora"), as ch01 89, ch02 9, 61, 71, ch04 39, 100, 104, ch05 79 (practice across the finals, written down 2026-11-17, copy-editor, ch05; Hart's Rules, generic noun). **One exception pending the author:** ch05 line 65, his own wording of 2026-11-17, "people of African heritage in the Diaspora", keeps his capital until he answers ch05-changes question 21.
- "the three strands" (awareness, knowledge, practice): defined in 00, referred to by name afterwards.
- Afrobeat: one word, capital A, no hyphen, no s, throughout (thesis majority "Afro-beat", 2537, 2603; once "Afro-beats", 2524). **Confirmed by the author 2026-11-14.**
- multiracial, multicultural (closed); multi-ethnic (hyphenated) (approved ch01 "multiracial"; ch02).
- Music genres lowercase and open as nouns: gospel, blues, jazz, soul, calypso, reggae, soca, hip hop, rock and roll; "rhythm and blues" at first use, R&B after. work-songs (Book Bible key term) (ch02).
- Place names in the author's stories (author 2026-11-17): "Port of Spain" (no hyphens); "Tranquility Boys Secondary School" (his spelling).
- "diaspora" is lower case everywhere, with no exception (author 2026-11-18: "spell 'diaspora' with a lower case 'd' here and elsewhere").
