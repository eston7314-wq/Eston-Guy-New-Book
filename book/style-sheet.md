# Style sheet

The house style for the whole manuscript, kept for the build and for a human proofreader.
The copy editor adds to it at each chapter. The same decisions are recorded in `book/book-bible.md`
under "Consistency decisions". If the two ever disagree, the Book Bible wins, and this sheet must be corrected.
Started 2026-11-11 by copy-editor at ch00. Each line gives its source.

## Spelling

- British, -ise (config rule 4): realise, recognise, organise, marginalised, programme, judgement, towards, learned (not learnt), cooperation (no hyphen), worshipping.
- practise (verb), practice (noun). licence (noun), license (verb).
- Proper names keep their own spelling: World Health Organization; US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. British spelling applies to our paraphrase of them.

## Punctuation

- No serial (Oxford) comma (config rule 4). If a list becomes ambiguous without it, recast the sentence. A comma before "and" joining two clauses, or before the second of two items, is allowed.
- Quotation marks: double for quotations and scare quotes, single inside them. The files use straight marks, which are curled at typesetting (the build does not do this yet). Punctuation goes inside the closing mark only if it belongs to the quotation. The citation follows the closing mark.
- No em or en dashes in prose (humanizer §8). Number ranges take a hyphen (Luke 8:43-48; 3,500-4,600).
- Abbreviations have no full stops: UK, USA, US, CDC, WHO, PhD, GCSE, KJV, ROI.

## Capitalisation

- Headings: H1 chapter titles in title case. H2 and H3 in sentence case. Closer labels are fixed: Reflection and practice; For church leaders; For families; For clinicians; Questions for small groups.
- Specific posts are capitalised (Head of Religious Studies, Head of Year 11, Assistant Headteacher, Heads of Year). The generic role is lowercase (the headteacher, a leader at her church, pastor).
- Named courses and programmes are capitalised: Contemporary Religion, Religious Studies, the Gifted and Talented programme.
- Faith terms: the Bible, Bible study, the New Testament, the Eucharist, Psalms (the book), the Gospels (the books), God, Christ. Lowercase: scripture, the psalms (individual psalms), biblical. "Black Church" is used only for the institution (Book Bible 2026-11-07).
- Cross-references: chapter 4 (lowercase mid-sentence, no leading zero; never "chapter 04"); Part One to Part Four; Appendix A, Appendix B; the preface, the afterword.

## Numbers and dates

- Words for one to ten in ordinary prose. Figures for 11 and above.
- Figures for every count, statistic or survey figure in a data sentence, even when the number is below 11 ("20 in the UK ... 30 women and 20 men"; "215 students").
- Survey proportions: "N of the 50" (Book Bible v2). Never "representative" or "balanced".
- Spell out a number that begins a sentence ("Fifty people...", "Twenty of them...").
- "fifty" in words when it names the group in narrative with no data in the sentence ("what fifty people told me", "the fifty"), to match the ch10 title.
- School years: Year 11. Dates: 21 April 2025 (day month year, no comma, no ordinal).
- Phone numbers are written as the service writes them: 116 123; 988; 9-8-8.
- Proposed, not yet used: "per cent" in running text, % in tables and figures.

## Scripture

- Translation: KJV/AKJV wording until the author chooses (Book Bible 2026-11-07).
- Reference form: book name in full, chapter:verse, translation after a comma, all in one pair of parentheses after the quotation: "thy faith hath made thee whole" (Luke 8:48, KJV). No abbreviated book names (Hebrews, not Heb.; 1 Corinthians, not 1 Cor.).
- Every quotation carries its translation until one translation is chosen for the book. Then the copyright page names it, and only quotations from another version are marked.
- "The writer to the Hebrews", not "Paul", for Hebrews (Book Bible 2026-11-07).

## Names

- Initials are spaced, with full stops: Eston T. B. Guy; Father F. W. G. Perkins; H. Gould; T. Hartney. Professor Salvatore Fava; Pope Francis.
- Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953; 2009 ed.), never "(1889)" (Book Bible v2).
- Samaritans (no "the" before it in the help-line note); 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline.

## Terms and hyphenation

- African heritage (umbrella term). African American, African British, African Canadian: no hyphen, as noun or adjective. Afro-Caribbean and mixed-race: hyphenated.
- mental health (noun); mental-health (before a noun: mental-health services). Likewise public-health bodies, long-term physical illness.
- well-being (always hyphenated). daughter-in-law. headteacher (one word).
- Survey wording: "50 participants", "overall numbers" (config rule 10).
- "the three strands" (awareness, knowledge, practice): defined in 00, referred to by name afterwards.
