## Reflection and practice

Read Luke 8:43-48 slowly, and then read it again with Mark 5:25-34 beside it. Notice what the woman had already tried, what she did when she heard of Jesus and what she did when he asked, "Who touched me?" Then ask yourself one question: is there some help I have been holding back from, a doctor, a counsellor or a friend, because I fear that reaching for it would show weak faith? If there is, name one step you could take this week, and ask God to go with you as you take it. You might end with Psalm 46:1: "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."

### For church leaders

- When you preach on the healing miracles, say plainly that the woman had already been to the physicians (Luke 8:43), and that seeking medical help can be part of reaching out in faith.
- Never tell someone who is still ill that they lacked faith. The nine lepers who did not return were cleansed all the same.<!-- note: copy edit 2026-11-16, author's follow-up decisions: open-claims 65 "cut" (author 2026-11-16): the clause "and Paul's thorn stayed after he prayed three times" and its flag deleted; nothing else in the closer depended on it. Paul's prayer and the answer stay in the body (line 37, 2 Corinthians 12:8-9). 05-final/ch04-changes.md -->

### For families

- If someone you love is unwell, help them make the appointment and keep it, and pray with them before and after.
- Read the story of Jairus's daughter with your children (Luke 8:41-56). The woman's story sits inside it, and Jesus says to the frightened father, "Fear not: believe only".

### For clinicians

- A patient of African heritage may speak of prayer and treatment as rivals. Ask what their faith says about seeking help. The story of the woman who "had spent all her living upon physicians" may open that conversation.
- Hope in the life to come can comfort a patient. Listen also for hope that has become a reason to put up with harm or to put off care.

### Questions for small groups

1. The woman came "trembling" when Jesus asked, "Who touched me?" What makes it hard for people in our church to come forward and say they are struggling?
2. All ten lepers were cleansed "as they went", before they could see it. What does acting in faith before you can see the outcome look like in your life?
3. Jesus cried, "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" How can a church make room for that kind of question?
