## Faith that puts down roots can outlast trouble and worry

## Reflection and practice

Bring to mind one person you find it hard to forgive, or one person you fear will never forgive you. You do not have to do anything about it today. Pray the Lord's Prayer slowly, and stop at "forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors". Say that person's name to God, and say honestly what they did, or what you did. Forgiving may take a long time, and it never means going back to someone who is still harming you. Then read the parable of the Lost Son (Luke 15:11-32) and ask yourself which of its three people you are closest to today: the son in the far country, the father watching the road or the brother outside the door.

### For church leaders

- When you preach on forgiveness, say in the same sermon that forgiving a wrong does not mean excusing it or staying where someone is being harmed, and say where people can find help.
- Be the father who goes out. Make sure that a young person who has been cut off, over a pregnancy, drugs or anything else, has someone in the church who will reach out to them and to their family.

### For families

- If you have closed the door on a son, a daughter or another relative, read the parable of the Lost Son with someone you trust, and ask what one step back towards them would look like.
- Teach children the Good Samaritan by living it. Notice together who in your street or your church is being passed by, and do one kind thing for them.

### For clinicians

- A patient of African heritage may feel that their faith requires them to forgive quickly. Ask what forgiveness means to them, and whether anyone is pressing them to forgive.
- If a patient speaks of forgiving someone who is still hurting them, treat it first as a question of safety.

### Questions for small groups

1. Jesus prayed "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do" while he was still on the cross. What makes forgiveness hardest for you, or for our communities?
2. Luke 17:3 asks us to rebuke a wrong and to forgive when there is repentance. How can a church help people do both?
3. In the parable of the Lost Son, the elder brother "would not go in". Who might be standing outside the door of your family or your church today?
