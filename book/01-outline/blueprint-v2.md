## Verdict first

The book still holds a real, unoccupied position, and v2 makes it stronger. None of the five verified comparables covers the UK, the USA and Canada together, and none reads the New Testament texts one by one. None joins colonial history, gospel music and an honest account of harm in a single book for lay readers. The book can now add a fourth asset: what 50 people of African heritage in those three countries reported.

That asset is **real but small, and it must be handled carefully**. Fifty self-selected people, reached through church networks (4456-4460), cannot speak for anyone else. Three of the 21 survey scales have their answer options out of order (Fig.5, 7, 8). The thesis's headline claim, that more practice goes with less anxiety (4658-4663), rests on statistical tests whose results are not reported. The yes/no questions on help-seeking were asked but never charted. Used plainly, as counts out of 50, the survey gives the book its most honest chapter. Overclaimed, it hands any reviewer the easiest possible attack. Ch10 is built for the first outcome.

The strongest single finding is a gap. 38 of 50 say their faith and practices help them manage mental-health challenges (Fig.13). Only 23 of 50 call their church community supportive about mental-health issues, the lowest score in the survey (Fig.19). The book names this **the listening gap**, and it is what turns the book from diagnosis to remedy.

## The promise (one sentence)

This book shows people of African heritage, and the families, church members and professionals around them, how the teachings, practices and community life of the New Testament can support mental health alongside professional care, and how to recognise and refuse the ways the same faith is sometimes used to wound.

- one author story;

## Part One: Roots

### 01. A Faith Handed Down in Chains

**Word budget:** 4,500 (unchanged)

**Essential arguments**
1. The faith arrived as a companion of empire. The Great Commission (Matthew 28:19-20) became a mandate to "civilise", and Ephesians 6:5 ("Servants, be obedient...") was used to justify slavery (3749-3770).
2. Enslaved Africans did not simply receive the faith; they took it back. They read Exodus as deliverance, Galatians 3:28 as equality and the Kingdom of God as a counter-story to racial hierarchy (3772-3781, 3686-3690, 4029-4031).
3. Missions differed. The Moravians taught spiritual equality yet worked within slavery; the colonial churches served the empire's economy (3828-3853, 3906-3916). The Spanish Franciscans, the Puritans and the Oregon missions (3855-3921) shrink to one paragraph: they are not about African-heritage communities.
4. The mental cost. Forced conversion repressed African ways of coping and promised an afterlife while ignoring present trauma: "spiritual alienation, guilt and mental anguish" (3940-3976). The trauma outlives slavery (1749-1761, 3672-3677, 3993-3998).
5. From "invisible institution" to the Black Church: Richard Allen, Frederick Douglass, Dr King and liberation theology (James Cone). The church became both refuge and resistance (1284-1295, 1564-1583, 3678-3709, 4008-4046).

**Owns / does not:** owns the history of mission and slavery, the Black Church's origins, Dr King, liberation theology, Galatians 3:28 and Ephesians 6:5. It does not tell how spirituals were made (ch02 owns that; 3781-3786 duplicates 2458-2462, so cut it here and point forward). It does not describe the Black Church's community work today (ch06). It does not report what participants said about historical trauma (ch10 reports that theme, 4711-4715, and refers back here).
**Shift:** "Christianity was the slave-master's religion, so it can only hurt us" / "Our faith has nothing to do with that history" -> "The faith was used against our ancestors and reclaimed by them; holding both truths is part of being well."
**Story slot:** must prove that this history lives on in families. Source candidate: 98-101 (Father Perkins first inspired the author to study religion). Also needed: [AUTHOR STORY NEEDED: how Christianity came into your own family line, such as a grandparent's church in the Caribbean or Africa, or a family memory of being told to "obey"]. **Open the chapter on the story, not on a date** (see Structural risks).
**Hook into ch02:** What the enslaved could not say aloud, they sang.
**Promises made:**
- We will hear how these same scriptures became songs of freedom (paid off in ch02).
- The trauma named here will be met with practical care, not left as history (paid off in ch08).

### 02. Songs for Sorrow

**Word budget:** 4,000 (unchanged)

**Essential arguments**
1. Music "expresses what cannot be put into words, and that which cannot remain silent" (2430-2431). Enslaved Africans blended their masters' hymns with African song into spirituals, for comfort and for group support (2446-2464).
2. Gospel was born of the spirituals and spread through the Great Migration, through Thomas Dorsey, Sister Rosetta Tharpe and Mahalia Jackson, and through the Gospel Music Workshop of America (2454-2494).
3. It crossed the Atlantic three ways:
   - With Windrush it came to Britain, where the London Community Gospel Choir and Liverpool's lockdown choir carried it on (2496-2530).
   - In the Caribbean it met calypso and reggae, as in Marley's "emancipate yourself from mental slavery" (2532-2552).
   - In Canada it is celebrated at the GMA Covenant Awards (2554-2566).
4. Song served as protest and as resilience, in the civil rights era and in activism since (2568-2581).
5. Today, Afrobeats and R&B bring younger and mixed-heritage people back to the old hymns. Music is social connection that eases isolation (2524-2530, 2597-2605).

**Owns / does not:** owns spirituals, gospel's history and the Marley line. It does not cover hymn-singing as a weekly spiritual practice (ch05 owns that, with Colossians 3:16) or Dr King (ch01).
**Shift:** "Gospel music is a Sunday style or entertainment" -> "Singing has been a mental-health practice for our people for four hundred years, and still is."
**Story slot:** must prove a song can carry a person through. Source candidate: 2515-2530, the Liverpool Lighthouse choir in lockdown (verify). Also needed: [AUTHOR STORY NEEDED: a hymn or gospel song that carried you or your family through a dark time, or a school choir or assembly moment from your teaching years].
**Hook into ch03:** The songs carried the teachings of Jesus; now open the teachings themselves.
**Promises made:**
- These songs drew on the teachings of Jesus, which the next chapter opens (paid off in ch03).

**Flags:**

### 04. Thy Faith Hath Made Thee Whole: Miracles, Letters and Hope

**Word budget:** 4,300 (v1: 4,700; -400 from the eschatology section)

**Essential arguments**
1. The miracles are signs, and faith sits at their centre ("thy faith hath saved thee", 2001-2008). The healing stories encourage "the personal search for spiritual support **alongside** medical treatment" (2014-2018). This is the source sentence for the whole book's position; give it weight.
2. The woman with the issue of blood and the ten lepers show faith as **intentional**: acting before the evidence arrives (2084-2105). Give the author's own definition of faith (2135-2140), with Hebrews 11:1.

## Structural risks

### The three biggest

1. **The numbers are small and imperfect, and ch10 can overclaim.** Fifty self-selected, mostly churchgoing people; three scales with out-of-order answer options (Fig.5, 7, 8); an association claim (4658-4663) with no reported statistics; and the help-seeking yes/no counts never charted. The danger is a chapter that sounds like a study proving that faith heals. Mitigations:
   - only the safe-figures table, every number "of 50", no association stated as fact;
   - the chapter's argument rests on the gap between two plain counts (Fig.13 and Fig.19), which needs no statistics;
   - the author supplies the missing yes/no counts if they exist.
2. **A dark, citation-poor middle.** Part Three (07-09) is now 13,600 consecutive words on guilt, illness, suicide and abuse (v1: 14,400). It is also where the source is least secure: more than 25 in-text citations in 2607-3591 have **no entry in the bibliography** (listed under each chapter). Mitigations:
   - each chapter ends on a church response that works (deliverance paired with care, the London and Toronto programmes, BCDAF, Brittney's second church);
   - researcher verification before drafting, and a clinician read of ch08 and ch09;
   - **new in v2:** ch10 follows straight after, with a calmer register and an opening on what helps (argument 3 before argument 4). That gives the reader air before the turn to practice.
3. **Thin story base, plus expansion pressure.** About 27,700 usable source words must become 51,700 without fresh drafting. Apart from the acknowledgements, the thesis has one named personal story (Brittney, 3462-3480), and rule 10 now closes the participants as a story source for good. **Eleven of thirteen story slots need an author story**, ch06 asks for an optional one, and ch09 deliberately has none.
