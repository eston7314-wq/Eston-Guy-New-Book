| Term | Verified gloss | Source | Added |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mental health (WHO) | A state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the stresses of life, realise their abilities, learn and work well, and contribute to their community. **Exact wording to re-check** ("learn and work well" or "learn well and work well") | WHO fact sheet "Mental health" (formerly "Mental health: strengthening our response"), https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-strengthening-our-response ; search only, page not opened | book-researcher, 2026-11-10 |
| Mental health (CDC) | "the component of behavioral health that includes our emotional, psychological, and social well-being". The thesis's "think, feel and act ... make healthy choices" lines are older CDC wording (archived 2012 page), not WHO | CDC Mental Health Data Channel, https://www.cdc.gov/mental-health/about-data/ ; search only | book-researcher, 2026-11-10 |
| Mental health (CDC), update | "Mental health includes our emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, and act. It also helps determine how we handle stress, relate to others, and make healthy choices." **Current cdc.gov text**, not only the 2012 archive (supersedes the note in the CDC row above). Variant on the CDC heart-disease page (15 May 2024): "how we think, feel, act, and make choices" | CDC "About Mental Health", https://www.cdc.gov/mental-health/about/index.html (legacy URL /mentalhealth/learn/); search-level, page not opened | book-researcher (ch00 fact check), 2026-11-10 |
| Mental health (WHO), update | Same wording found again, "learn well and work well" in WHO EB156/8 (15 Jan 2025) and WHO/Europe fact sheet; split with "learn and work well" persists in search summaries. Paraphrase safe; quotation needs the page opened | https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/EB156/B156_8-en.pdf ; search-level | book-researcher (ch00 fact check), 2026-11-10 |
| Chronic illness and mental health | Link runs both ways: chronic physical conditions raise the risk of depression or a mental-health condition; depression raises the risk of diabetes, heart disease and stroke. NICE: depression about 2-3 times more common with a chronic physical health problem | CDC "About Mental Health"; NIMH https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/chronic-illness-mental-health ; NICE CG91 https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg91/chapter/Context ; search-level | book-researcher (ch00 fact check), 2026-11-10 |
| Meaning as use (Wittgenstein) | For a large class of cases, though not all, the meaning of a word is its use in the language. *Philosophical Investigations* §43 (1953; 4th ed. Wiley-Blackwell 2009). Not "(1889)", his birth year | SEP, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/ ; search, 2026-11-10 | book-researcher, 2026-11-10 |
| "Thy faith hath made thee whole" | KJV Luke 8:48 (also Mark 5:34, Matt. 9:22). Greek *sesōken*, from *sōzō*, "save, heal, deliver"; some modern versions render it "saved" or "healed" | KJV text via kingjamesbibleonline.org; lexical note from search results (Bible-study sites), 2026-11-10; check a lexicon before ch04 relies on it | book-researcher, 2026-11-10 |
| Invisible institution | The hidden worship of enslaved people, held out of the enslavers' sight (Mary Prince describes a prayer shed of boughs on Turks Island pulled down twice). Title of Raboteau, *Slave Religion: The "Invisible Institution" in the Antebellum South* (OUP, 1978). Often traced to E. Franklin Frazier, *The Negro Church in America* (1963 or 1964): do not write that he coined it (Wikipedia only) | Raboteau: library and Cambridge Core records; Prince 1831 (opened); search-level otherwise | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
| Great Commission | Jesus's charge in Matthew 28:19-20: "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them ..." (KJV spells "baptizing", "alway"). Missionaries read it as a mandate to convert; the thesis says it became a mandate to "civilise" (3756-3760), which is a historical claim about how it was used, not what the text says | KJV 1769, two GitHub files, opened | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
| Slave Bible | *Select Parts of the Holy Bible, for the Use of the Negro Slaves, in the British West-India Islands* (London, 1807), an abridged missionary Bible with most of Exodus cut. Museum of the Bible exhibit 2018-19 with Fisk University. Its framing is disputed (Hicks-Keeton, *The Revealer*, 2020) | museumofthebible.org; therevealer.org; search-level | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
| Black theology / liberation theology | Black theology: James H. Cone, *Black Theology and Black Power* (1969), *A Black Theology of Liberation* (1970); God sides with the oppressed. Latin American liberation theology: Medellín 1968, Gutiérrez 1971. They are **contemporary**; do not write Black theology as derived from Latin America (thesis 3680, 3815) | EBSCO Research Starters; Wikipedia; search-level | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
| Moravians (Unitas Fratrum, Herrnhut) | Protestant church renewed at Herrnhut, Germany, under Count Zinzendorf. First missionaries to enslaved Africans: Dober (potter) and Nitschmann (carpenter), St Thomas, 13 Dec 1732. Taught spiritual equality but did not challenge slavery (thesis 3906-3916). "Sold themselves as slaves" is a myth | Wikipedia; Christian History Institute; Penn State UP sample chapter; search-level | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
| Historical trauma / intergenerational trauma | Historical trauma: a community's shared memory of past collective harm, carried as a public narrative that can feed distress and resilience (Mohatt et al. 2014, lead). Intergenerational transmission: well described through family and social routes; **epigenetic (biological) inheritance not demonstrated in humans** (Yehuda and Lehrner 2018, lead). Do not write "trauma in our genes" | ch01-evidence.md S3, S4; abstracts only, not opened | book-researcher (ch01), 2026-11-11 |
