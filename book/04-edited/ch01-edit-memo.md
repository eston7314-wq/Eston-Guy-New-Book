## What was wrong, and what I did about it

The draft was in good shape. All five v2 arguments were there, the voice was close to ch00 and the evidence was mostly honest. The problems were structural, and three of them were small factual errors that a sceptical reader with the primary texts could catch.

1. **Two headings did not cover their sections.**
   - Section 4, "The wound is carried in memory, story and present-day racism", opened with 150 words of Canadian colonial history, then spent 600 words on the mental cost *under* slavery (Henry, Covey, Prince's fretting). The heading covered only its last third. It is now **"The mind paid a price, and the wound outlived slavery"**. The section's first sentence, "The mind paid a price.", would then have repeated the heading (humanizer §24), so it is gone.
   - Section 3, "The Moravians taught equality before God and still worked within slavery", opened on the Anglicans, Catholics and Baptists and closed on the Spanish, Puritan and Oregon missions, so the heading covered only the middle. It is now **"Missions differed, and even the Moravians worked within slavery"**. That is v2 argument 3 as a claim the section proves. "The missions were not all alike." restated the new heading and was cut.
2. **Paragraphs in the wrong place.**
   - *Canada* moved to section 1, before Fanon. It is about empire spreading Catholicism and Anglicanism "to strengthen their hold over land and people", so it belongs with "The faith arrived in the company of empire". Its 1834 date now answers that section's first sentence. I kept the source's point that this racism "added to their anguish" (thesis 3945-3948), which the draft had dropped.
   - *Spanish, Puritan and Oregon missions* moved from the end of section 3 to second place, before "The Moravians were different in one important way." The contrast now comes after all the missions it is contrasted with. The signpost "a short word on them is enough" is cut. The dates are compressed ("beginning with San Diego in 1769"; "Methodists and Presbyterians reached Oregon in the 1830s") so the paragraph no longer reads as a list of dates. Cut-list row 36 records the end year 1782, the Willamette Valley and the two separate Oregon years, which now sit only in the hidden note.
   - *Dr King before Cone.* The draft introduced King as "The best-known voice of that tradition" straight after the paragraph on Black and liberation theology. King was not a voice of Black theology, and Cone's 1969 book came after King's main work. King now follows the NAACP and civil-rights paragraph ("The best-known voice of that movement was..."). Cone follows him, and Cone's closing line ("Both movements have had to keep disentangling...") now leads into "The same double edge runs through our churches in Britain and Canada today."
3. **Three statements the primary texts do not support (checked against the opened Gutenberg files).**
   - Douglass: "When readers took his attacks on the religion of the slaveholders as an attack on all religion, he wrote an appendix to correct them." The appendix was not a reply to readers. Douglass wrote that his tone "may possibly lead those unacquainted with my religious views to suppose me an opponent of all religion". Now: "Douglass worried that his attacks on the religion of the slaveholders might be taken for an attack on all religion, so he added an appendix". The note records the check.
   - Prince: "The English Church was slower to welcome her." Her text says she "had been baptized long before this, in August 1817, by the Rev. Mr. Curtin, of the English Church". The English baptism came *before* the Moravians. Now: "The English Church was more guarded. It had baptised her in 1817, but..."
   - Prince: "The Moravians taught her, married her and sheltered her." In London they took in her trunk. They did not house her. Now: "taught her, married her and stood by her in London".
4. **Evidence honesty in the mental-cost argument (v2 argument 4).**
   - *A credential the author has not claimed.* "I hold that as a belief, from my reading and my years of pastoral work." Nothing on record says the author held a pastoral post in a church. Ch00 shows pastoral care in schools. The sentence also repeated the "I believe" that opened the paragraph. Cut. The belief sentence stands on its own "I believe".
   - *The studies are matched to the part of the claim they bear on.* The draft's "Researchers have looked at parts of it" let two studies of *present-day* racism seem to support the legacy of slavery. Now: "Studies point most clearly to one part of it, the harm done by racism today." The next paragraph opens "How the older wound reaches people today is less certain."
   - *The counter-evidence is now faced head-on.* The draft reported Williams et al. 2007 and then stated its interpretation as fact: "The harm shows most in how long suffering lasts and how rarely it is treated." Now: "One large American survey points the other way. ... If that holds, none of us should say that our people are more depressed because of slavery. It suggests real strength, perhaps partly drawn from faith, and harm that shows less in how often we fall ill than in how long suffering lasts and how rarely it is treated." The survey is now placed "in the USA", so UK readers do not take "Black Caribbeans" to mean people living in the Caribbean.
   - The section still says nothing about trauma in our genes ("You may have heard that trauma is written into our bodies, and I do not claim it"). All five studies keep their `[UNVERIFIED: study not opened ...]` flags, and no study numbers appear.
   - King: "That is my own view; I know of no study that has measured it" spoke for the author's reading. It is now "That is my own view, not a measured finding."
5. **Liberation theology and Cone.** "Both drew on the New Testament's teaching of love and equality, and on Galatians 3:28, to confront racial injustice" claimed a focus on race for Latin American liberation theology, whose subject was the poor. That sentence now belongs to Black theology alone. The paragraph opens on Cone and "the Black experience in America", gives Medellín and Gutiérrez as "At the same moment", and keeps "Cone's first book came out before Gutiérrez's, and the two movements grew up side by side". No reader can now take Cone to be derived from Latin America. "A Black Theology of Liberation in 1970" became "the year after", one date fewer.
6. **The shift was only half paid off.** "Holding both truths at once is part of being well" was stated in paragraph 3 and never came back. The last paragraph now ends: "Douglass and Mary Prince kept their faith and told the truth about what was done in its name. I believe that honesty is good for the mind as well as the soul. What the enslaved could not say aloud, they sang." The same paragraph used to say "The faith they received had come to the Caribbean through empire". That placed the maternal Anglican line in the Caribbean, which the author did not say. It now reads "Those churches had travelled with empire, and their roots ran back to the plantation."
7. **Smaller corrections.**
   - "the empire turned to Africa for workers" was a euphemism. Now "for people to enslave" (source: "for slaves").
   - "Moses goes to Pharaoh with four words" was wrong: Exodus 5:1 says more. Now "Moses carries God's demand to Pharaoh".
   - "Black Christians have invoked this verse ever since" became "have often invoked" (source: "frequently").
   - King and the Acts: "The Civil Rights Act followed in 1964" gave chronology only. It is now "his principles influenced the passing of", the source's causal claim (thesis 1575-1577).
   - "did the same work on a larger scale" (Kingdom of God) was vague. Now "gave them a whole story to set against the plantation".
   - "even though enslaved Africans ... had their own ways" was illogical. Now "to people who already had their own ways".
8. **Flow and length.**
   - The 280-word Douglass paragraph is split in two: Auld, then the Sabbath school.
   - The Covey and Prince paragraph is split before "She also refused...".
   - "After the *Narrative* appeared, he travelled to Britain and Ireland" used to close section 2 on a travel fact. It moved into Douglass's introduction. Section 2 now ends on "I love the pure, peaceable, and impartial Christianity of Christ."
9. **Cuts that do not need rows.** All are duplicates, or pack material not taken from the source:
   - "and justified slavery" in section 3 repeated section 1.
   - "Bethel ... whose church was dedicated in 1794" came from the pack, not the thesis. The author may want it back.
   - "Douglass put the whole matter in one sentence" introduced two sentences.
   - "If it helps," in the reflection.

If Mode B cannot open Paradies 2015, Wallace 2016, Mohatt 2014, Yehuda and Lehrner 2018 and Williams 2007, the fix is already set out. Keep the author's belief sentence.
