## For the copy edit: noticed, not fixed

1. **"Marley's line sits close to the question of this book."** On a read-aloud I stumble here: the sentence names "the question of this book" without stating it, and the reader has to wait two sentences ("I believe faith can help with that work of the mind"). Stating the question would mean choosing the book's question in words the text does not yet use, which risks a meaning change, so I left it. The copy editor, or the author, may want to name it.
2. **"Further north the story was the same. In Canada, as in the USA, ..."** says "the same" twice in two sentences. "Like in the USA" is the thesis's (3965). The first sentence is the dev edit's transition, so I left it.
3. **Closer bullets:** all six open with a short imperative label sentence ("Tell the story behind the songs.", "Pass the songs down.", "Ask about music." ...), and in three of them the next sentence repeats the label. Ch01's closer does this in two of six. This is formatting by rule at most (§19 without bold), and it is *weak alone*. I left it because the closer's form follows the config, but the copy editor may vary one or two.
4. **Serial comma in the reflection:** "a hymn from childhood, a gospel song from a hard year, or a chorus someone in your family sang around the house". This is a three-item list with a comma before "or" (config rule 4: no serial comma). Punctuation, not a humanizer pattern.
5. **"Marley calls on his listeners to free their own minds..., and he calls what he is singing songs of freedom"**: "calls" twice in one sentence. Word repetition.
6. **"lively expression of faith that enlivened"**: the source's repeat (2504-2505). Vary it or keep it, as the dev memo says.
7. **"Afrobeat" (lines 45, 61; closer) and "Afrobeats" (line 67)**: an open spelling question from the draft notes, for the style sheet.
8. **"That is a belief, drawn from history and from the songs themselves."** The dev edit cut "the songs say so in their own words" because no song is quoted. "drawn from ... the songs themselves" is milder and is the author's ground for his belief, but the fact checker may want to look at it beside that cut.
