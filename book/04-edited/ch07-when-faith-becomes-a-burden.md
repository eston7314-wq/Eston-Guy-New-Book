## Reflection and practice

Read Hebrews 4:15 slowly this week: "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin." If you are struggling, write down what you have been telling yourself about why: that it is your fault, that it is an attack or that you must keep it quiet for your family's sake. Write the verse beside it. Then tell one person you trust what you wrote, and ask them to help you think about the next step, whether that is prayer, a talk with your pastor or an appointment with a doctor. If you are not struggling, think of someone who may be, and of one thing you could do to make it easier for them to speak.

### For church leaders

- Teach plainly, and more than once, that mental illness is not moral failure. When you preach on John 9, read the disciples' question aloud as well as Jesus's answer, so that your people hear the question asked and settled.
- When you pray for deliverance with someone who is distressed, ask also whether they have seen a doctor, and offer to help them go. Prayer and treatment can sit side by side in the same week.

### For families

- If someone in your family is struggling, do not keep it inside the family for the sake of the family's name. Ask together who could help, inside the church and outside it.
- If one of you has seen a relative sectioned, talk about what happened and why, so that the next person who needs urgent care is not afraid to ask for it.

### For clinicians

- Ask your patient what they believe is causing their distress, and listen to the answer before you offer your own. If a patient fears that treatment will cost them their faith, say plainly that you are not asking them to give it up.
- Remember that mistrust of services may have been learned from experience. Ask gently whether the patient or their family has had care before, and what it was like.

### Questions for small groups

1. The disciples asked, "Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?" Where do we still hear that question in our church, out loud or in silence?
2. Have you known someone who kept an illness quiet for the sake of their family or their church? What would have made it easier for them to speak?
3. What would it look like for our church to pray for someone and help them find professional care in the same week?
