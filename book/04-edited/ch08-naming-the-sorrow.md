### For church leaders

- Preach on these illnesses by name. When you speak of anxiety, depression, trauma, addiction or schizophrenia, say in the same sermon that a doctor can help, so that no one hears it as a choice between the two.
- When someone in your congregation is struggling with drink or drugs, restore them gently, as Galatians 6:1 asks, and find out before you need to which recovery services near you will work with a church.

### For families

- You may be the friend or relative who points the way: if someone's distress after a terrible event does not ease, help them to see a doctor.
- If someone in your family hears voices or speaks of visions, listen before you decide what they mean, and go with them to the doctor as well as to prayer.

### For clinicians

- Ask a patient of faith what their church teaches about their illness, and what they make of it themselves. A patient who believes their voices may come from God needs to know that you will not dismiss their faith.
- Where a patient's church runs a recovery programme or a prayer group, ask, with the patient's consent, whether it could sit alongside the care you give.

### Questions for small groups

1. Which of these sorrows do we find hardest to name in our church, and why?
2. James asks that patience "have her perfect work" (James 1:4). How can a church stay patient with someone whose recovery is slow?
3. What would it take for our church to restore someone "in the spirit of meekness" (Galatians 6:1), and to work with the doctor or the recovery service that is helping them as well?
