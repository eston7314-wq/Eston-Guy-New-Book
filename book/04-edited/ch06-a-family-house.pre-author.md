## Reflection and practice

Read Galatians 6:2 slowly this week: "Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ." Then ask yourself two questions. Whose burden could I help to carry, and who could help to carry mine? Write down one name for each. For the first, choose one small, practical thing you can do in the next seven days: a phone call, a visit, a meal, a lift to an appointment. For the second, think about what you would need to tell that person, and pray for the courage to tell them. If what you are carrying is more than a church friend or your pastor can help with, ask them to help you find someone who can.

### For church leaders

- Know where your door leads. Keep a short, up-to-date list of local doctors, counsellors and mental-health services, so that when someone comes to you first you can walk with them to the next door.
- Look at your congregation with the risk factors in mind. Ask who is isolated, who is without secure housing or work, and who is meeting racism at work or at school, and build your support groups, visits and meals around them.
- Plan for the night of a crisis and for the months after it. Grenfell showed what opening the doors at 3 a.m. can mean, and how long the need for follow-up care can last.

### For families

- Let your church family into the hard seasons of your home, and let your children see you both help others and accept help.
- If one of you is struggling, ask together who at church you trust, and whether that person could help you look for professional care as well.

### For clinicians

- Ask your patient whether their pastor or a church leader was the first person they told. With the patient's consent, that person may help you understand what the patient is facing and may help them keep to their care.
- Remember that for many patients of African heritage the church may be a protective factor, and for some a place of shelter from the racism they meet elsewhere. Ask how it helps them, and treat it as part of their support.

### Questions for small groups

1. Acts 2:42 names four things the first believers "continued stedfastly" in: the apostles' teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread and prayer. Which of these is strongest in our church, and which needs the most care?
2. When has someone helped to carry a burden of yours? What made it possible for you to let them?
3. If a fire, a death or a sudden crisis struck our neighbourhood tonight, what could our church open its doors to offer, and what help would we need to find beyond our own walls?
