## What was wrong, and what I did about it

The draft was in good shape. It carries all five blueprint v2 arguments, its scripture is exact KJV, its benefits are written as belief, the woman's story opens it and pays ch03's hook, and the shift ("Faith is reaching out, and reaching out can include a doctor") is stated in the opening and earned at the landing. What was wrong was in four places. Two objections a sceptical reader would raise were not met. One paragraph argued against its own heading. One heading promised less than its section delivers. The rest is wording: four "I believe" frames that config rule 5 does not need, two "resonates", and some repetition.

1. **The strongest objection to the shift was not met.** The opening quotes Mark 5:26: the doctors left her "nothing bettered, but rather grew worse". A reader who holds "prayer or the doctor" will take that as the point of the story: the doctors failed and Jesus did not. The evidence pack names this as counter-evidence 8.2. The draft answered it only indirectly ("nothing in her story tells anyone to stop seeing a doctor"). Now the position paragraph names it and answers it: "Mark says they left her worse, yet nothing in her story blames her for going or tells anyone to stop seeing a doctor" (B3). The answer comes before Luke 5:31, so Jesus's own picture of the physician closes the reply.
2. **An expert would catch the faith sayings.** The paragraph says Jesus put "repeated emphasis on faith as the basis of healing" and gives "Thy faith hath saved thee" (Luke 7:50) as the example. But Luke 7:50 is said to the woman whose sins he has just forgiven (7:48), and there is no healing in that scene. And three of the five "thy faith hath made thee whole" references (Matthew 9:22, Mark 5:34, Luke 8:48) are his words to the woman in this chapter's opening. That is the strongest support the title has, and the draft did not say so. Both points now go into the paragraph (B2), using only the references already cited.
3. **Section 2 argued against its own heading.** Under "Faith acts before the evidence arrives", John 10:38 has Jesus telling his challengers to "believe the works": believe because of the evidence. In the thesis the verse supports "knowledge is an essential element of Christian faith" (2122-2126), which is a point about signs. I moved the paragraph unchanged to section 1, straight after "The Gospels present the miracles as signs of the power of God". There it says what the signs are for, and section 2 now runs from the lepers to Hebrews 11:1 without contradicting itself (B1).
4. **Section 3's heading covered half the section.** "Hope in the resurrection can hold a grieving heart" covers Lazarus, Nain and Jairus. The second half (Romans 8:28, 1 Peter 1:13, Jeremiah 29:11, Psalm 46) is about hope in God's promises among people living "with anxiety and stress". The heading now names both (B12), so every H2 is a claim its section proves.
5. **Wording.** There were 16 "I believe" frames in the prose (17 counting the Apostles' Creed). Four are gone where rule 5 does not need them: a doctrine now framed as "Christians hold" (B13), an inference now argued from his own line (B14), a moral charge that is not a claim of fact (B17), and "I believe the belief" (B5). "resonates" goes from four to two (B15, B16), and back-to-back "In my view" openings are varied (B19). Five repetitions are tightened (B4, B7 to B11), and one sentence is moved back to its thesis place so that a paragraph about Paul does not end on Jesus (B18).
