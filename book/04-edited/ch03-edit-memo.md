## What was wrong, and what I did about it

The draft was close. It carried all five blueprint v2 arguments, its scripture was the KJV, its benefits were written as belief, and the story pass had given it a real opening scene. What was wrong lay in three places. Six pieces of the author's source were still out of the chapter, and he has said they stay in. Two sentences in section 1 had lost the logic they had in the thesis when the draft moved things around them. And two phrases were ambiguous in a way a careful reader would notice.

1. **The six kept cut-list rows were not in the chapter.** All six are now back, at the places listed in table A. Rows 1-4 (thesis 1903-1915, "Hope and Redemption") had been held for ch04. By the author's decision they stay in ch03. The best place for them is section 5, after the elder brother and before the story slot. The parable itself says twice that the son "was dead, and is alive again" (Luke 15:24, 15:32), and the opening of the chapter names the thought that someone has "gone too far ever to come home". The New Testament's message of redemption answers that thought, so it belongs with the parable that carries the chapter's landing. Placed there, the order is: parable, the hope behind it, the author's own story (when he gives it), then the landing. Row 3's sentence is already used word for word in approved ch02, so ch03 refers back to it in a paraphrase and does not repeat it.
2. **Section 1 had lost two of the thesis's joins.** In the thesis, "Yet, Jesus' teachings evoke a type of soul searching" answers "these sermons go against the grain" (1874-1877), and "Accordingly, the emphasis on unconditional love" follows "eliminates racism" (1884-1886). In the draft, "Yet" followed the Black theology sentence, so it contrasted with nothing, and the sentence on racism inside the church had been put between the racism sentence and "Accordingly". Both joins are repaired (table B, rows B3 to B5).
3. **The strongest objection inside the chapter's own lines is now where it cannot be missed.** A sceptic reading "I believe this attitude of compassion and forgiveness can help to break down racism" will answer that the church has not broken down its own. The thesis says so itself (3984-3985), and the draft had the sentence, but it said "The church has had to learn it too", which implies the lesson is learned. It now ends section 1 as "The church has not always followed Jesus in this", straight after the woman at the well, where Jesus "did not take up the old line between their peoples". The guardrail on forgiveness and abuse (section 2) and the counterweight to "take no thought" (section 4) were already met, and are unchanged.
4. **Two ambiguities.** "These two commandments" came right after Mark's first and second commandment (love God, love your neighbour), but it means love of neighbour and love of enemy (thesis 1871-1874). And "went further" was used twice within a few sentences (table B, rows B1 and B2).

## Weakest remaining section and its fix

**Section 5, from the elder brother to the landing.** The blueprint says the story slot "must prove that reconciliation happens". Without it, the section moves from a parable, through the hope behind it, straight to "I believe there is always a way home". The belief is the author's to state, but a sceptical reader has been given no case where it came true, and the chapter's last paragraphs depend on one. The new redemption paragraphs make the gap a little wider, because they add a second statement of hope before any evidence of it. The fix is the author's reconciliation story in the slot, which he has said he will supply. The slot is untouched, and nothing in the chapter pretends it is filled.

Second, for the record: the hope paragraphs add one more "scripture, then benefit" step (blueprint v2 warns of this pattern in chapters 03-06). They are short and sit inside a parable section, so I have kept them as they are.
