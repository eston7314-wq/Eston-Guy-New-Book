## Reflection and practice

Read Ephesians 5:21-25 slowly this week, and notice what the passage asks of each person in a marriage. Then read Romans 8:38-39. If these pages have stirred something painful, you do not have to carry it alone: talk to someone you trust today, and use the help lines at the start of this chapter if you need them now. If they have not, ask God to show you one way your church could become the second church for someone.

### For church leaders

- When you speak of suicide, from the pulpit or at a funeral, say what several Protestant church leaders have stressed, that those who die by suicide are not beyond God's grace and mercy, and name the help lines in the same breath.
- Learn the signs my thesis lists, and know before you need it where you will send someone: a doctor or GP, a crisis line or the emergency services.
- If someone tells you they are being abused, put their safety first and help them reach the police or a domestic-abuse service. Never use Ephesians 5:22 or Matthew 5:39 to send them home. Ask whether you and your leaders can be trained to respond.

### For families

- If someone you love withdraws from everyone or speaks about death, stay close, ask how they are and help them talk to a doctor.
- If someone in your family is being hurt at home, whether a man or a woman, help them reach safety first; questions about the marriage can wait.

### For clinicians

- Ask a patient of faith what their church has taught them about suicide. Someone who fears for their soul, or for a relative who has died, may welcome a faith leader's help alongside yours.
- If a patient says a pastor or a verse has told them to stay with a partner who hurts them, take the danger seriously, and ask, with consent, whether someone at their church could help them get to safety.

### Questions for small groups

1. What does our church teach, out loud or in silence, about people who die by suicide and about the families they leave behind?
2. How would a husband who lived by Ephesians 5:25 treat his wife?
3. What would our church need to learn, and whom would we need to know, to be the second church for someone being hurt at home?
