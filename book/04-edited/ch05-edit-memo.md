## What was wrong, and what I did about it

The draft was in good shape: all five blueprint v2 arguments, exact KJV, benefits framed as belief, Gethsemane as the opening and the bridge, the guardrail named once, and the hook into ch06 word for word. What was wrong was in five places.

1. **Half of the shift was never met.** Blueprint v2's shift runs from "Prayer is all I need" / "Prayer is wishful thinking" to "real practices of care". The draft answers the first well (James 5:14-15, "They do not replace treatment", the guardrail). It never names the second. A sceptical reader reaches the umbrella paragraph ("None of these studies shows that prayer or worship causes better mental health") and keeps the chapter: if the studies cannot show cause, prayer is wishful thinking. The chapter already holds the answer in its own scripture. Jesus prayed for the cup to be removed and still went to the cross; Hannah ate again before anything had changed. Neither prayer rested on the wish coming true, and what changed was in the person praying. I added one paragraph that says so (B12), using only what the chapter already quotes. This is the edit that matters most.
2. **Two passages did not prove the heading they sat under.** Section 1 ("A spiritual practice is something a believer does again and again to draw near to God") opened with a paragraph on scholarly attention and "Research shows" (flag 66), so the definition came second. That paragraph is about the research, and it now opens section 5 (A1). Section 4 ("Worship, song and service draw us out of ourselves and into company") ended on church-based care joined with professional services and a pointer to ch11. That is the "alongside other help" half of the shift line, not worship. It now sits in the landing, directly before the shift line, so the line is earned on both halves: company (worship, James 5:14, Gethsemane's three friends) and other help (the guardrail, then these two sentences) (A2, B10).
3. **One heading covered half its section.** "Scripture read slowly and often can give a weary mind rest" did not cover the Polanyi paragraph, which is blueprint argument 3's second half (faith is known by practising it). The heading now names both (B6).
4. **The promise was buried.** "Prayer is not always enough on its own, and I will be honest about when it is not" ran on through a colon into two chapter pointers. It is now its own sentence, and the next names chapter 8 as where each condition is set out (B11). The Book Bible promise (05 -> 08) is made in the reader's words.
5. **Accuracy and repetition.** "I wrote in chapter 4 that faith gives hope and a sense of purpose": ch04 does not say that in those words. It now says what ch04 says (lines 43 and 61) (B3). The second "I wrote in chapter 4 that" in the same section is varied (B5). The "as I use the term" definition (thesis 970-978) repeated the preface callback's subject three paragraphs later; it is joined to it (B2). Polanyi's step to mental well-being ("and so is good for") is now stated as Eston's reasoning (B8). Three over-full paragraphs are split (B4, B7, B9). One writer's sentence that argued against section 4's heading is out (B9).

## Weakest remaining section and its fix

**Section 5, "Studies report that faith goes with better mental health ..."** It now carries seven of the chapter's nine flags (66, 68, 70-74) in the 530 words before the umbrella paragraph, at the point where the sceptic is reading most closely. Of the four named studies, under the rules only the UK half of Larcom can be tied to a real work, and its citation is wrong in year, lead author and co-authors. Smith 2003 is not identified, Koenig 2009 has no bibliography entry, and Ali and Thompson 2019 cannot be found at the cited issue and pages. Moving flag 66 here (A1) puts the chapter's evidence problems in one place, which is honest, but it also makes them visible. The section is saved by its own last paragraphs: the umbrella paragraph and B12 make the chapter's case without leaning on any study. The fix is Eston's: (1) identify each work (open-claims 70, 71, 73, 74) or accept the researcher's candidates; (2) where he cannot, the section shrinks to his own summary ("In my thesis I summarised the research this way ...") plus the association caveat. That would take out about 280 words and leave the argument intact. (3) Recast the unnamed attributions (66, 68, 72) as his observation where they are his.

Second, **the Polanyi paragraph.** His account says tacit knowledge "must be put into language or models before others can understand it" and that people "build knowledge by working with others, in groups and in organisations". To an expert reader this sounds more like later writing on knowledge in organisations than like *Personal Knowledge*. The pack could not check what Polanyi says (rule 2), so this is a question for Eston (question 3), not a correction. The first and last paragraphs of the chapter do not depend on it.
