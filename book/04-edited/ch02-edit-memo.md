## What was wrong, and what I did about it

The draft was close. It covered all five v2 arguments, its sources were honest and its voice matched ch00 and ch01. What was wrong lay in three places. It answered only half of its strongest objection. Section 1 had its paragraphs in the wrong order. And it said the same thing too often: the "comfort, protest, keep going" purpose appeared six times, and the "nothing was measured" disclaimer twice.

1. **The counter-evidence was only half met.** The pack (section 8.1) gives two thesis counterweights. One is forced hymn-singing (3958-3961). The other is Christianity as "a tool of control, pacifying and suppressing their desires for freedom" (3966-3970). The draft met the first and left out the second, and the second is the one a sceptic would use against this chapter: perhaps the comfort of a hymn was part of the control. Both lines are already in approved ch01, so this chapter refers back and does not retell them.
   - Before: "In chapter 1 I wrote about enslaved people who looked for ways to refuse forced hymns and forced baptisms. The spirituals show what others did with the same hymns. As I read it, they kept the words of faith..."
   - After: "In chapter 1 I wrote about enslaved people who looked for ways to refuse forced hymns and forced baptisms, and about others for whom Christianity became a tool of control that pacified them and suppressed their desire for freedom. That leaves a hard possibility: that the comfort of a hymn was part of the control, a way of making suffering bearable so that no one rose against it. As I read it, "Go Down, Moses" answers that. People who sang the story of a slave people led out of Egypt were not singing themselves into silence. They kept the words of faith..."
   - The answer is framed as the author's reading ("As I read it", config rule 5). It quotes nothing from the spiritual and uses only the Exodus story that ch01 already tells. A new hidden note records the sources.
2. **Section 1 was in the wrong order, so "This is how the Exodus became song" pointed at Canada.** The ch01 promise ("Chapter 2 tells how they turned it into song") is paid in this section, but the payment came after a paragraph about Canada. The new order is: 1619, then the spirituals, then the Exodus and "Go Down, Moses" (so "This" now points to the blending of hymns into spirituals), then Canada (which now opens "Further north the story was the same."), then the counterweight, which can now call on "Go Down, Moses", then the reader paragraph and the bridge to gospel.
3. **The chapter kept saying the same things.**
   - *The purpose triad.* The reader paragraph ended "...people the law called property, and they sang them to lift their spirits, to stay strong and to protest." That was the fifth statement of the purpose in one section, and "the law called property" is ch01's image (ch01 line 81). It now reads: "The words in your mouth were first sung by people in chains."
   - *The disclaimer told twice.* The paragraph that closed section 4 ("The record shows that our ancestors used these songs to comfort one another, to protest and to keep going, and the songs say so in their own words. When I call that a way of caring for the mind, I am speaking as a believer who has read the history, and I have measured nothing. Researchers have studied...") repeated the opening's "That is a belief... Nobody in my research measured it". It also claimed "the songs say so in their own words", which the chapter cannot show, because it quotes no song. I cut the paragraph. Its one new point, that studies exist and have not been weighed, moved into the opening's statement of the evidence, with its hidden note. The reader now hears once, early and plainly, what is belief and what was measured. Section 4 ends on the author's own line, "a powerful tool for social transformation, and for building resilience".
   - *Section 4.* Before: "Many spirituals and gospel songs were sung during the civil rights movement in America... Chapter 1 told that movement's story through Dr King; here I add only that it had its songs. I think it matters that they sang. People singing together in a crowd can find a courage that each of them might not find alone, and some of the songs they sang were spirituals, carried down from the plantations." After: "Chapter 1 told the story of the civil rights movement in America through Dr King. The movement also had its songs. Many spirituals and gospel songs were sung in those years, when activists of different races campaigned for justice and equality, and the spirituals among them had come down from the plantations. I think it matters that they sang. People singing together in a crowd can find a courage that each of them might not find alone." The signpost "here I add only" is gone, the spirituals are mentioned once instead of twice, and the courage sentence now carries the paragraph's point.
   - *"Hope and redemption" twice in a row.* It closed one paragraph and opened the author's 1910-1912 sentence in the next. It now stays only in his sentence.
4. **Smaller logic faults.**
   - The opening said "I follow the song out of the plantations and into the churches", and paragraph 4 then began "I start from the church". It now reads "The church is where the songs have lived."
   - "I closed the last chapter there" pointed back four sentences to ch01's line. Cut, because the first sentence already makes the join.
   - "It became possible when African Americans moved..." The "It" read as gospel. The thesis means the blending of styles (2473-2474). It is now "That blend became possible...".
   - Section 2 never connected with the rest of the chapter. One sentence now does: "So the music travelled first with people who had left home, as it would again." This sets up Windrush and the migrants' churches in the last section.
   - The Caribbean section broke Marley in two: the artists and Marley, then "still growing", then back to Marley. "Still growing" now closes the first Caribbean paragraph, so the two Marley paragraphs run together.
   - "Moreover," opened the last section with nothing before it to add to. Dropped. His "Above all" and "Furthermore" stay.
   - "They used musical instruments and spirituals to comfort themselves... The rhythms comforted them" said comfort twice. The first one is gone; the idea stays.
5. **The songs paragraph claimed more than it showed.** Before: "These are simple songs, and none of them argues a case. They were sung to people whose days were hard. Many churches..." After: "These are simple songs. Two of them name Jesus outright, as the one who taught the singer to smile and the one who washed the singer's sins away. Churches across the diaspora..." The claim about Jesus's teachings now rests only on the two songs whose paraphrase names him. It says nothing about "Together Again", whose lyric the author paraphrased without Jesus in it.
6. **The joins around the Gladesmore story (the story itself is untouched).**
   - Lead-in. Before: "The one I can tell of is my own, from my years as a Head of Year in North London." This put "Head of Year" in the sentence just before his "As their Head of Year". After: "The prayer I can speak for is my own, from my teaching years in North London."
   - The join after it now draws out what his story actually shows: he thanks God *and* names everyone whose work made the results. That ties his story to the book's promise (preface: "Prayer and professional care can work together"). It also heads off a reader who might hear "God's rewards" as the "if your faith were stronger" theology that the preface and Part Three criticise. Before: "My prayer for those students and the songs in this chapter come from the same faith. Our people sang what they had learned from Jesus through slavery, migration and lockdown." After: "In that story my prayer sat beside the work and support of many people, among them the students, their teachers and mentors, their parents and the Headteacher. That is how I hold faith all through this book, beside the other help we need, and it is the same faith our people put into song. They sang what they had learned from Jesus through slavery, migration and lockdown." The people named are the ones his story thanks. Nothing is added to his account.

## Weakest remaining section and its fix

**The songs paragraph in the last section** ("Listen to the words of the songs..." through "...the hymns and spirituals"). The chapter's claim that the songs carried the teachings of Jesus rests on three paraphrased fragments. One of them, "Together Again", is a pop song whose paraphrase does not mention Jesus, sitting among "songs our people have turned to when their days were dark and dreary". The author decided to include it, and I have kept it. The edit now rests the claim about Jesus on the other two songs only. A sceptical reader may still ask why the song belongs here. The cheapest fix is for the author to say, in a clause of his own words, why that song is on his list (for example, what it meant to him or to people he knows). I have not written that clause and have not inserted a flag, because his decision stands. **Question for the author.**

Second, for the record, not a recommendation: the Gladesmore story is about prayer, not song. The 1910-1912 sentence before it ("hymns, prayers and gospel music") and the new join after it hold it in the chapter. It stays where it is, by his decision.
