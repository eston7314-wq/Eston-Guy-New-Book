# From Thesis to Book: Conversion and Publishing Plan

**Source:** *Do Religious Awareness, Religious Knowledge, and Religious Practice Derived from New Testament Christianity Influence the Mental Health of People of African Heritage Living in the UK, USA and Canada?* (PhD in Religion, Selinus University, 2025). Eston T. B. Guy.
**Size of the current draft:** about 38,600 words including front matter, the table of contents and the bibliography. It has 7 chapters, 27 survey charts and 50 participants.
**Method used:** the `long-form-content-frameworks` skill, treating the book as an "ebook/multi-chapter" format with a *narrative arc* spine and *layered argument* sections.

---

## 1. The core decision: which book is this?

A thesis is written to satisfy examiners. A book is written for readers. You have to choose your reader first, because that choice drives the structure, the length, the publisher and the marketing.

| | **Route A: Trade / church-audience book** (recommended) | **Route B: Academic short monograph** |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Pastors, church leaders, Christians of African heritage and their families, counsellors, chaplains | Researchers, theology and psychology lecturers, libraries |
| Length | 45,000–60,000 words (you need to add more stories and practical material) | 25,000–50,000 words (close to the current length) |
| Tone | Warm, pastoral, story-led, with the evidence kept in the background | Formal and citation-heavy, with methodology in the main text |
| Publishers | Wipf & Stock (Cascade Books), SPCK (UK), InterVarsity Press, Judson Press, Fortress Press, or self-publishing | Palgrave Pivot, Routledge Focus, Cambridge Elements (Religion), Wipf & Stock (Pickwick) |
| Strength | Biggest audience and impact. It matches the thesis's own goal of "culturally sensitive mental health support". | Academic credibility, and the current length already fits |

**Why Route A:** the material that stands out in this thesis, and that readers can't easily find elsewhere, is:
- the gospel-music history (USA, UK, Caribbean, Canada);
- the honest chapter on how faith can *harm* (guilt, fear of witchcraft, "spiritual warfare" language, stigma);
- the colonial and missionary history;
- the voices of 50 participants across three countries.

That mix speaks most directly to churches and families. Also, with a sample of 50, the statistics can't carry a heavy academic monograph on their own, but they work well as *evidence behind a pastoral book*. Academic presses also look closely at the awarding institution, and that is another reason the trade or church route is the stronger first move.

A hybrid is possible: publish the trade book, and later turn Chapters 6–7 into a peer-reviewed journal article (for example in *Mental Health, Religion & Culture* or the *Journal of Religion and Health*).

## 3. What to cut, keep and add

### Cut or move to appendices ("de-thesising")
- **Declaration, the full abstract, "Aim / Objectives / Research Questions", and "Structure of the Thesis" (1.2–1.7).** Replace them with a short preface.
- **Chapter 5, Research Methodology.** Shrink it to a 2-page "How this research was done" note, with the full version in an appendix.
- **Chapter 2, Literature Review.** Spread the best of it through the other chapters and drop the stand-alone survey. Cut most of the epistemology material (rationalism and empiricism) or condense it to one page.
- **The 27 figures.** Keep 5–7 that carry the argument, such as faith importance, prayer as coping, faith's positive impact, stigma and help-seeking, and church supportiveness. Put the rest in an appendix or on a companion website.
- **Repeated "Conclusion" sub-sections** at the end of every section. Remove them.

### Keep and strengthen
- Chapter 3A (the teachings of Jesus, the parables, the miracles and the apostolic letters, read as mental-health resources)
- Chapter 3B (both positive and negative impacts, plus the gospel music history)
- Chapter 4 (colonisation, missionaries and resistance)
- Chapters 6–7 (the findings), rewritten around the participants' voices

### Add (this is what makes it a book)
- **An opening scene.** Use a moment from your teaching years in North London or a composite interview story. Don't open with a definition. (Skill rule: the first page must earn the reader's next 30 minutes.)
- **Participant stories.** Use anonymised stories and quotes from the interviews, at least one per chapter.
- **Your own voice.** Bring in your 30+ years as Head of Religious Studies, Head of Year and Assistant Headteacher.
- **Practical sections.** Close each chapter with "For church leaders", "For families" and "For clinicians", or put reflection questions at the end of each chapter.
- **A practical final chapter.** For example: "A Toolkit for Mental-Health-Friendly Churches", covering how to talk about mental health from the pulpit, when to refer someone to professionals, and how pastors and GPs or therapists can work together.
- **Signposting resources** for the UK, USA and Canada (helplines and Black-led mental health organisations).

Skill checks to apply while rewriting each chapter:
- Each chapter should open with a scene, a story or a contested claim.
- Each chapter should end with something specific to do or think about, not a recap.
- Around Chapter 6/7 (the halfway point), restate the central thesis: *faith heals when it is understood, and harms when it is misused or used in place of care*.
- Keep chapters roughly balanced. No chapter should be more than 3× the length of another chapter with similar weight.
- Use a visual element (chart, table, sidebar or pull quote) every 600–1,000 words.
- Choose one citation style for the general reader (endnotes) and use it consistently.

## 5. Things to check before publication

1. **Participant consent.** Check that the interview and survey consent forms covered *publication in a book*, not just the thesis. Anonymise all stories fully. Where the consent doesn't cover it, use composite or illustrative cases.
2. **Copyright on song lyrics.** Gospel lyrics after about 1928 are usually still in copyright, so you need permission or a short paraphrase. Spirituals and traditional hymns are generally public domain.
3. **Bible translation.** Name the translation you use. Most modern translations allow quotations up to a limit, as stated on their copyright page. In the UK the KJV is under Crown copyright, but quotation is normally fine with attribution.
4. **Images and the university logo.** Remove the logo. Use only images you own or have licensed.
5. **Rights to your own thesis.** Check Selinus University's policy and any repository deposit. Usually the author keeps the rights, but confirm this.
6. **Mental-health safety.** Add a short safeguarding note at the front, saying the book is not a substitute for professional care, and give crisis line numbers for the UK, USA and Canada.
7. **Professional review.** Ask a clinician (a psychiatrist, psychologist or counsellor) to read Chapters 7–8 and 10 for clinical accuracy.

## 6. Publishing plan and timeline (about 12 months)

### Phase 1: Positioning (Month 1)
- Make the final choice of route and title.
- Write a **one-page book proposal**: the hook, the audience, 3–5 comparable titles, an author bio (your teaching career, your PhD, your community work) and the chapter outline from Section 4.
- Look at comparable titles to position against. For example, books on Black churches and mental health, Christianity and depression, and gospel music history. Check what exists in Christian bookshops and on Amazon in each country.

### Phase 2: Rewrite (Months 2–6)
- Rewrite one chapter roughly every two weeks, in this order: Chapter 1 first, then Chapters 3 and 7 (your strongest material and best for sample chapters), then the rest.
- Each pass should cut thesis language, add stories, and add a practical close to the chapter.
- Recruit 3–5 **beta readers**: a pastor, a mental-health professional, a lay church member of African heritage, and someone in each of the US and Canada.

### Phase 3: Choose the publishing path (start in Month 3, in parallel with the rewrite)
**Option 1: Traditional / Christian publisher**
- Send the proposal, Chapter 1 and one strong chapter (for example Chapter 3 or 7).
- Good first targets that are known for publishing revised doctoral work and diaspora theology: **Wipf & Stock (Cascade/Pickwick)** and **SPCK**. Next try **IVP**, **Fortress**, **Judson Press** and **Langham Publishing** (for global church audiences).
- Most of these accept proposals without a literary agent.
- Expect 2–4 months for a decision and 9–15 months from contract to print.

**Option 2: Self-publish (fastest, and you keep full control and higher royalties)**
- Hire a professional **developmental editor**, then a **copy-editor**, then a **proofreader**. Use separate people if the budget allows.
- Commission a professional cover design. Have the interior typeset as a paperback, an ebook and possibly a hardback.
- Buy your own **ISBNs**: Nielsen (UK), Bowker (USA), or free through Library and Archives Canada if you are resident there.
- Distribute through **IngramSpark** (bookshops, libraries and churches worldwide) plus **Amazon KDP** (ebook and print), with optional Apple, Kobo and Google Play.
- An **audiobook** read in your own voice would suit this audience well. You can produce it through ACX or Findaway Voices.
- Rough budget: £2,500–£6,000 / US$3,000–$7,500 for editing, cover and typesetting.

### Phase 4: Production (Months 7–9)
- Editing rounds, the clinician review, permissions, the final charts (redrawn for print and readable in black and white), the index and the cover.
- Collect endorsements from 3–5 people: pastors, Black mental-health professionals and academics.

### Phase 5: Launch (Months 10–12)
- **Church networks:** Black-majority and Pentecostal church networks in the UK, the Black church denominations in the USA (AME, COGIC, Baptist conventions) and Caribbean-Canadian congregations. Offer free talks, sermons and workshops. Bulk orders for church study groups often outsell retail.
- **Study guide:** a free downloadable small-group guide (one session per chapter). This drives bulk sales.
- **Media:** Christian radio such as Premier Christian Radio (UK), Black-community radio and press, faith and mental-health podcasts.
- **Events:** launch events in London, plus at least one virtual launch for US and Canadian audiences. Also target conferences such as faith and mental-health conferences, chaplaincy events, NHS and community mental-health trusts, and theological colleges.
- **Online:** a LinkedIn and social media series serialising short pieces from each chapter. Use the `linkedin-content-planner` and `content-repurposing` skills for this.
- **Spin-offs:** a journal article from the findings, a workshop for clergy, and possibly a short booklet such as *Ten Things Every Pastor Should Know About Mental Health*.
