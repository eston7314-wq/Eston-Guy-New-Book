# Publishing route: options and recommendation

Prepared 7 November 2026 for Eston T. B. Guy. Target launch: **May 2027**, which is about six months away.
Fees and rules below were checked online in November 2026. Confirm them on each company's own site before you sign up or submit anything.

## The deciding factor: the May 2027 date

Six months is enough to edit, design and self-publish a 50,000-word book. It is **not** enough to get there through a traditional publisher. That route usually takes several months to win a contract and then about a year more to reach print, so a traditional deal would most likely mean a 2028 launch. If May 2027 matters most, self-publishing is the realistic route.

---

## Option 1: Self-publish under your own imprint, through KDP and IngramSpark (recommended)

You become the publisher. You can name your own small imprint, for example "[Your name] Press". You hire the editor and the cover designer, and two print-on-demand services print and distribute the book.

| | Amazon KDP | IngramSpark |
|---|---|---|
| What it covers | Kindle ebook and paperback on Amazon worldwide | Paperback and hardback for bookshops, libraries, church bookshops and online retailers outside Amazon |
| Set-up cost | Free | Free since February 2026, provided the files meet their requirements. Revisions are also free now. |
| Your earnings | Ebook: 70% if priced between $2.99 and $12.99 (this band has applied since July 2026), otherwise 35%. Paperback: 60% of list price at $9.99 or above (50% below that), minus printing cost. | You set the retailer discount. Printing cost comes off, plus a market-access fee that rose from 1% to 1.875% in 2026. |
| One rule to know | For the 70% ebook rate, the ebook must be priced at least 20% below the print edition. | Bookshops usually expect a 40–55% discount, and they prefer books they can return. |

**Typical way to combine them:** sell the Kindle ebook and the Amazon paperback through KDP. Use IngramSpark for everything outside Amazon, including a hardback if you want one.

**What you pay for:** the platforms are free, so the money goes on professional help. A rough budget is £2,500–£6,000 (about US$3,000–$7,500), covering:
- a developmental edit;
- copy-editing and proofreading;
- the cover design;
- interior layout (typesetting);
- ISBNs. Nielsen sells them in the UK and Bowker in the USA. They are free in Canada if you live there.

The Book Agency agents can handle much of the editing groundwork. A human copy-editor and proofreader are still strongly advised.

**Pros**
- Meets May 2027.
- You keep full control and all rights.
- You earn the highest share of each sale.
- You can buy copies cheaply for church events and talks.

**Cons**
- You pay the costs up front.
- Getting into physical bookshops is harder.
- You do all the marketing yourself, which church networks can make up for.

---

## Option 2: Self-publish first, then approach a traditional publisher (good second step)

Launch in May 2027 as in Option 1. Build sales and church contacts over the first year. Then offer a revised edition, or your next book, to a Christian publisher with real sales figures behind you. Your rights stay with you, so this path remains open.

---

## Option 3: Traditional Christian publisher (later launch, most prestige)

The publisher pays for editing, design and printing, and gets the book into bookshops. You earn a smaller royalty, and the publisher sets the timetable.

| Publisher | What I found | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Wipf and Stock** (Cascade Books imprint), USA | Accepts proposals directly through a proposal form. They publish an Author Guide (version 10, October 2025). Cascade is their imprint for religious books that are scholarly but readable for a wide audience. | Strong fit. They regularly publish books developed from doctoral research. |
| **Darton, Longman and Todd**, UK | Considers unsolicited proposals, but says only a small number of its books have come from them. | Good UK fit for warm, pastoral, ecumenical books. |
| **SPCK**, UK | Its FAQ says nearly all its books come through literary agents. | You would probably need an agent first, which adds more time. |

**Pros**
- No up-front cost to you.
- Bookshop distribution and the publisher's name behind the book.

**Cons**
- Most likely misses May 2027.
- Lower royalties.
- Less control over the title, cover and edits.
- Acceptance is not guaranteed.

---

## Avoid

Avoid "assisted self-publishing" or "vanity" companies that charge thousands of pounds for a package, take a cut of your royalties, or keep rights to your book. Everything they sell you can buy separately through Option 1, for less, while you keep full ownership.

---

## Recommendation

**Go with Option 1 now, and keep Option 2 open for later.**
1. Self-publish in May 2027 under your own imprint: KDP for Amazon, IngramSpark for everywhere else.
2. Buy your own ISBNs so that you, not Amazon, are the registered publisher.
3. Hire a human copy-editor and proofreader for the final pass.
4. After a year of sales, decide whether to approach Wipf and Stock or Darton, Longman and Todd.

**Decision needed from you:** reply with Option 1, 2 or 3. If you pick 3, the launch date will need to move.
