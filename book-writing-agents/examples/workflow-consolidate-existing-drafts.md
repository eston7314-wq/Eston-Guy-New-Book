# Workflow: consolidating existing drafts

> A book that already existed as roughly 200,000 words of AI-assisted drafts across four files, with duplicated chapter versions, chat commentary, and a "200,000 words per chapter" instruction baked in.

## Import (orchestrator, one session)

1. Located the drafts in the author's cloud storage; imported four files into `book/source/` with a provenance table and a per-chapter map of first-occurrence line numbers and version counts (chapter 4 alone had 25 headings across "Part N" and "Part N Expanded" rewrites).
2. Filled `book/book.config.md` from the author's own outline and prose. Target reader and credibility were inferred and marked so; the author confirms them before marketing.
3. Stored the author's structure verbatim as `book/01-outline/author-guidelines.md` and made it authoritative over any agent proposal.
4. Ran the Book Strategist in enrichment mode: per-chapter arguments, shifts, story slots (ten needed the author), hooks, budgets summing to 60,600 words, 36 promises and 70 named ideas into the Book Bible, and a structural-risks section that found two chapters two-thirds identical and three drafts of one closing chapter.

## Rules that came out of it

- Discard the source's word-count instruction (config rule 6).
- Never carry chat commentary forward (rule 7).
- Reframe metaphysical claims as tradition or belief, never delete the idea (rule 5).
- Nothing leaves the book without the author's sign-off; cut lists per chapter (rule 8).
- An untraceable attribution is a flag, not a removal (rule 9).

## When an editorial diagnostic arrived

The author supplied a third-party structural analysis proposing merged chapters and a re-sequenced arc. It was stored as a reference in every agent's read order, its useful parts adopted (per-chapter claim cards, a contradiction matrix as a standing developmental check), and its six conflicts with the author's guidelines listed for the author to rule on, with the author's structure as the default.
